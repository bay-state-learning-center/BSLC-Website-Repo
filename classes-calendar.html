2025–26 Classes

Our class lineup changes every year based on the needs and interests of our members, and is updated in the fall. This list reflects the 2025–26 year.

Math & Science

  • The Math Lab · Amelia Real math is about spotting patterns, visualizing relationships, and solving problems creatively. Each week we tackle a new puzzle, game, or thought experiment — from infinite monkeys to the Monty Hall problem to the mathematics of sunflowers.
  • Statistics · Amelia The mathematical study of data, with applications in science, economics, and everyday life. Members explore data visualization, probability, and statistical inference at their own pace.
  • Logic · Amelia The formal study of deductive reasoning — how we determine whether an argument's premises entail its conclusion. Covers logical connectives, complex sentences, and constructing proofs.
  • HiSET Math · Amelia For members preparing for the HiSET math exam. Covers ratios, percentages, algebra, geometry, and statistics through scaffolded problem sets and practice tests, with individualized support throughout.
  • Math Practice Hour · Amelia A collaborative work session for members building math fluency — whether for the HiSET or just for the love of it. Work on problem sets, tackle tricky concepts, and support each other in a relaxed setting.
  • Euclidean Geometry · George Members work their way through Euclid's Elements and practice presenting geometric demonstrations to the class. Part math, part public speaking, and part ancient cult.
  • Geology & Geography · George Drawing on George's background in volcanology, this course explores geological processes — plate tectonics, volcanic activity, landforms — and how physical changes to the earth have shaped human history and social change.
  • Sociology · Amelia The study of human culture, institutions, and group behavior. Topics are guided by member interest — past themes have included crime, inequality, race and gender, religion, labor, and economic systems.
  • Human Anatomy · Ava A survey of human anatomy — the body's major systems, structures, and how they work together.

Language & Literature

  • Intro to Poetry · JayJay A broad overview of how to read, analyze, and write poetry. Covers figurative language, poetic elements, and form — with a poetry sampler and seminar-style discussion each week, plus original student work.
  • Form Poetry · JayJay For students who've taken Intro to Poetry. Focuses on structured forms — haiku, sonnets, triolets, limericks — and poetic meter through scansion of everyday speech and longer works including Coleridge and Byron.
  • Playing with Poetry · JayJay A weekly creative practice with a twist: the obvious words are banned. Each class, members brainstorm — then eliminate — the expected vocabulary around a prompt, building specificity, flexibility, and fresh language.
  • Writing Workshop · JayJay Time set aside for members to work semi-independently on writing as a practice, with support tailored to individual goals.
  • Books and Tea · Terry A collaborative reading group where members choose the books and lead the discussion. Outside reading is required — listening on Audible or Libby counts.
  • Books and Protein Powder · JayJay Sci-fi read aloud, together. Members listen and read along as the group works through a science fiction text, stopping to discuss as they go.
  • Monsters, Gods, and Men · JayJay An interdisciplinary literature course exploring Prometheus Bound, Frankenstein, and Paradise Lost. Examines the ethics of creation, the cost of knowledge, and what horror reveals about what it means to be human.
  • American Sign Language · Rena (member) An introduction to ASL basics — signs, handshapes, and the fundamentals of communicating in a visual language.

Humanities

  • History: The Eurasian Steppe · George 5,000 miles of grassland from Hungary to China, and the countless tribes and empires it produced. A discussion-based course drawing on historians, writers, podcasts, and primary sources — members choose their own sources and come ready to share.
  • Humanities · George A year-long seminar built around member-selected books and short stories. Each session is a guided discussion of the text — what it says, what it means, and what it leaves open.
  • Philosophy and/or Religion · George This year the class focused almost entirely on Buddhist thought — its origins, core teachings, and various traditions. The "and/or" is intentional: the course follows the questions wherever they lead.
  • Critical Thinking: BS Armor · JayJay & Amelia Members practice analyzing contemporary media through the lens of rhetorical appeals, logical fallacies, statistical literacy, and how algorithm-driven content shapes our thinking.
  • Implications Discussion · George & JayJay Part free-association, part structured discussion — members deep-dive into tangential topics from across their classes and weave them into a coherent thread. Past topics have ranged from copyright ethics to JoJo's Bizarre Adventure.
  • Current Events · Terry News through the lens of media criticism — evaluating sources, spotting bias, and distinguishing reporting from propaganda. Members bring their own stories. Starts with the two NYT weekly quizzes.
  • Guilty? Mock Trial & Debate · Erin Senapati-Brown (volunteer) A crash course in law, debate, and critical thinking. Members build arguments, present cases, and analyze evidence from multiple angles — culminating in a full mock trial.

Arts & Making

  • The Visual Art of Storytelling · Keith Cantone (volunteer) From cave paintings to comic books. A course built entirely around the instructor's personal library — members share their own favorites and opinions, while Keith brings as many books as his Prius can handle.
  • Music · Jay Weekly band practice for BSLC's rock bands. Jay works with each group on their material, helping members develop as musicians and as collaborators.
  • Photography & Image Editing · Terry Technique, lighting, and the art of making images — from smartphone to digital SLR. Members also learn Photoshop basics and explore digital art through image editing and multimedia projects.
  • 3D Printing & Design · Jacob (member) Members learn to set up a printer, prepare models for printing, and design their own objects using Fusion — then print them.
  • Handwork Hour · Terry A collaborative making session. Everyone brings their skills to share — it's not about what you make, it's about the pleasure of working with your hands alongside others.
  • Dungeons & Dragons · Rowan (member) & Amelia Part improv theater, part board game, part math class in disguise. Rowan creates original adventures; Amelia guides gameplay and logistics. Open to new and experienced members alike.
  • Magic: The Gathering · Neko (member) An introduction to Magic: The Gathering for new players — how to build a deck, understand the rules, and actually win a game.
  • LARPing · Jay, Ava & Rowan (member) Live-action roleplay — world-building, character creation, and collaborative storytelling that gets everyone off their chairs and into the story.
  • Game Design · Adam (volunteer) The basics of designing and coding computer and video games.
  • Drawing · Lee (volunteer) An introduction to drawing techniques for beginners (or anyone who wants to hone their skills).

Life & Transitions

  • Life Skills & Financial Literacy · Terry Members develop a personal plan for educational and career decisions, with guest speakers sharing their own paths. Topics include budgeting, résumés, job interviews, mental and physical health, and finding your direction.
  • Transitions Program · Terry For members in their final year at BSLC. Weekly check-ins, HiSET prep, college and community college guidance, and internship and mentorship opportunities — all aimed at making the next step feel less daunting.
  • HiSET Test Prep · Terry An individually tailored program pulling from BSLC's full course offerings. Covers test-taking strategies, practice tests, and scheduling — with the goal of earning a HiSET certificate by year's end.
  • Inquiry Projects · Terry Each member picks a big idea, question, or project and dives deep. Some do research; others make something. Past projects have included curating an art portfolio, launching an Etsy shop selling handmade goods, and researching the best freshwater fishing spots in Massachusetts.
  • Nature · Terry The whole point is to get outside. Hiking, fishing, growing plants in the Tower Garden, cooking projects, documentaries, indigenous ecological knowledge, and whatever else the class dreams up.
